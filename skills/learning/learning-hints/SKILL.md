# Hints

Help the learner make the next useful move. Use the existing attempt to locate the obstacle; ask for working only when it is needed to diagnose that obstacle.

1. Identify the last valid step and the missing decision. Begin with the smallest useful cue, such as the applicable condition or an overlooked quantity.
2. If needed, become more explicit: identify the relevant relation, outline the next operation, then demonstrate that operation. Let the learner supply the next step when interactive practice is the goal.
3. Respond to the new attempt. Avoid repeatedly restating the same cue. Persistent confusion needs a prerequisite explanation or [worked example](../learning-worked-examples/SKILL.md), not a longer interrogation.
4. Offer or provide a full solution when requested, or when continued hints are unproductive and the learner accepts that form of help. Finish with the unresolved step or the completion actually observed.

A direct-answer request overrides the default hint sequence. In a batch of practice questions, keep unused answers separate from the questions. Treat every hinted answer as assisted under [assessment](../learning-router/references/assessment.md). A fresh [retrieval](../learning-retrieval/SKILL.md) or [transfer](../learning-transfer/SKILL.md) question can later check independence.

A wrong solution needing diagnosis belongs in [error repair](../learning-error-repair/SKILL.md). Hints own live assistance, not scoring or a whole course plan. Read [GATE adaptation](../learning-router/references/gate.md) for GATE and [research](../learning-router/references/evidence.md) for what guarded AI-tutoring studies do and do not establish.
