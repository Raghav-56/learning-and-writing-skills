# Transfer

Change something that tests the principle rather than recognition of the old answer. Own generalization, not merely recalling the same solution.

1. Identify the source problem, underlying principle and the change to test: representation, constraint, assumption, goal or combination with a familiar prerequisite. Start with a manageable change; distant transfer is harder and is not automatic.
2. Construct and independently solve the new problem before presenting it. Check that the change really alters what the learner must notice. Label generated questions as original practice and keep the solution separate until the attempt or a direct request.
3. Assess whether the learner identified the relevant principle and adapted it correctly. Accept valid alternate approaches. Distinguish a missing prerequisite from a failure to generalize.
4. Explain the invariant and the changed condition after the attempt. Route a specific misconception to [error repair](../learning-error-repair/SKILL.md), or method-choice problems to [interleaving](../learning-interleaving/SKILL.md).

Changing only the numbers can be useful procedural practice, but does not by itself demonstrate broad transfer. Generated GATE-style questions are supplements; authentic PYQs calibrate exam content and difficulty.

End with the precise change the learner handled and any help used. Offer later [spaced retrieval](../learning-spacing/SKILL.md) if retention also needs checking. Read [assessment](../learning-router/references/assessment.md) before making a progress claim, [GATE adaptation](../learning-router/references/gate.md) for GATE, and [research](../learning-router/references/evidence.md) for the limits of transfer evidence.
