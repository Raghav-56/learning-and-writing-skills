# Exam practice

Match the practice to the actual exam and preserve a genuinely independent attempt when that is the goal.

1. Use the requested paper or verified source. Establish the section, time allowance and scoring rules from the paper or current official instructions. For a custom mini-test, state its practice rules explicitly and distinguish them from official rules.
2. Keep unseen questions and solutions held out until the learner is ready. During an exam simulation, keep answers separate and give no item-specific help. If help is requested, provide it and mark the affected item assisted rather than silently counting it as independent.
3. Use the learner's reported timing or actual tool observations. Chat timestamps do not establish focused solving time. If a hard timer is unavailable, ask the learner to use one; do not claim the session was timed automatically.
4. Score against a checked key. Separate wrong answers, unanswered items, invalidated questions and uncertainty about the key. Classroom partial-credit reasoning can be useful feedback, but official scoring follows the exam's actual format.
5. Analyse losses from knowledge, strategy choice, execution and pacing using observed work. Apply [error repair](../learning-error-repair/SKILL.md), then [interleaving](../learning-interleaving/SKILL.md) or [spacing](../learning-spacing/SKILL.md) to the specific recurring gap.

Before a mock, use known weak material for a short review rather than exposing held-out questions. With a very short time budget, reduce scope rather than launching a full diagnostic. Treat rank estimates and topic predictions as separate evidence-demanding requests.

Return the score with source and help conditions, observed timing limits, recurring losses and the next focused practice. Read [assessment](../learning-router/references/assessment.md) and [GATE adaptation](../learning-router/references/gate.md) before GATE scoring or logistics.
