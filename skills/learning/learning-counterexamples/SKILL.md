# Counterexamples

Own falsification and boundary conditions. A counterexample must satisfy the claim's premises while violating its conclusion.

1. Restate the claim precisely, with its quantifiers and assumptions. Distinguish finding one counterexample from proving that none exists.
2. Construct the smallest case that exposes the suspected failure. Check every premise and calculate or trace the conclusion. Reject examples that quietly break a premise.
3. Show exactly which implication fails. Ask the learner to predict the result first only when they want interactive practice; give the example directly when requested.
4. Refine the claim to a defensible version, or identify the assumption needed. A surviving claim remains unproved unless you provide an actual proof.

For example, test a greedy rule on a tiny graph or distinguish a necessary condition from a sufficient one. A finite simulation can establish a counterexample for its checked case, not universal truth.

Use [self-explanation](../learning-self-explanation/SKILL.md) to examine the mistaken reasoning. Use [transfer](../learning-transfer/SKILL.md) when the learner should apply the repaired rule elsewhere. These have separate purposes: falsify a claim here, assess generalization there.

End with the checked case and the corrected scope of the claim. Read [assessment](../learning-router/references/assessment.md) when judging the learner, [GATE adaptation](../learning-router/references/gate.md) for GATE, and [research](../learning-router/references/evidence.md) for the heuristic status of this method.
