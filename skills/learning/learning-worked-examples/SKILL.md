# Worked examples

Show a complete, technically checked procedure before asking a novice to invent it. Fade assistance according to observed performance, not a fixed number of examples.

1. State the problem, givens, assumptions and why the procedure applies. Work the smallest example that exposes the mechanism. Show intermediate steps and justify the consequential choices.
2. When practice is wanted, give a related example with a meaningful step omitted. Often omit a late step first, then remove more of the solution as the learner succeeds. Preserve enough context to make the task self-contained.
3. Ask for a short [self-explanation](../learning-self-explanation/SKILL.md) when a step could be copied without understanding. If the learner stalls, restore the missing prerequisite or a limited [hint](../learning-hints/SKILL.md).
4. Finish the practice sequence with a fresh independent problem or [transfer](../learning-transfer/SKILL.md) case. Record assistance explicitly; completing an exposed solution is not independent performance.

When the user requests only a worked solution, provide it and stop. When the procedure is already fluent, move directly to retrieval or mixed practice. Avoid making a skilled learner repeat elementary examples to satisfy a lesson template.

The output is the checked demonstration and, if attempted, evidence of what the learner completed after help was reduced. Read [assessment](../learning-router/references/assessment.md), [GATE adaptation](../learning-router/references/gate.md) for GATE, and [research](../learning-router/references/evidence.md) for example-fading evidence and its transfer limits.
